VIII

Part eight · a detailed study

Spiritual Warfare and Dominion

“We do not wrestle against flesh and blood” (Eph 6:12). This part covers who the enemy is and where Christ has put him, what authority, power and dominion mean, and what we may speak to and command, what it means to be seated with Christ as co-heirs, and how every enemy, down to death, is put under his feet. It also shows where people go wrong and how sons, priests and kings enforce a victory that has already been won.

Ephesians 1:20 → 2:6 → 6:12 · layered map

The battlefield: who is fighting, and from where

Paul describes where Christ is seated (chapter 1) and where we are seated (chapter 2) before he describes the battle (chapter 6). Select a layer of the map.

The four words of Ephesians 6:12

Ephesians 6 uses wrestling language (palē): close, personal and hand to hand. Notice the verbs, though: “be strong in the Lord”, “put on”, “stand”, “withstand”, “pray”. The chapter never sends the church off to hunt the enemy. It tells the church to hold ground that Christ has already taken.

The powers are real but subordinate. They are created (Col 1:16), disarmed (Col 2:15), made subject to Christ (1 Pet 3:22), and shown God’s wisdom through the church (Eph 3:10).

VIII·d

Ephesians 1:3 · 2:6 · Romans 8:17

Seated in the Heavenly Places, Co-Heirs with Christ

“Blessed … with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places” and “seated with him”. This part explains what our heavenly position means, how it works out on earth, our dual reality, and what being co-heirs with Christ changes for mature sons (huioi) now.

The shape of Ephesians · two-level diagram

Seated with him: sit, walk, stand

Ephesians moves from position to practice to power. Select each movement and watch heaven’s seat reach down to earth’s ground.

What “seated” means

Finished work

Priests in the temple never sat; their work was never done. Christ “sat down at the right hand of God” because the sacrifice was complete (Heb 10:11–12). Seated means rest.

What “seated” means

Throne and rule

To sit at the right hand is to share royal authority (Ps 110:1). We are seated “far above all rule and authority” in him (Eph 1:21; 2:6). Seated means authority.

What “seated” means

Union and access

“In Christ Jesus”: where he is, we are. Our life is “hidden with Christ in God” (Col 3:3), so we approach the throne with confidence (Heb 4:16). Seated means belonging.

Heirs who govern: the huios difference

“The heir, as long as he is a child, is no different from a slave, though he is the owner of everything” (Gal 4:1). The inheritance is already ours, but maturity decides how much of it we steward now. Sons (huioi) are “led by the Spirit” (Rom 8:14), trained by practice (Heb 5:14) and trusted with the Father’s business. See nēpios, teknon, huios in Part IX →

VIII·e

Ephesians 1:19–23 · Psalm 110:1 · 1 Corinthians 15:24–28

Far Above All: Every Enemy Under His Feet

The Father raised Christ “far above all rule and authority and power and dominion”. He put all things under his feet and gave him as head to the church, his body. This part explains what that means for us in Christ, in daily life and in our assignment, which enemies are being subdued, and how the body deals with the last enemy, death.

Ephesians 1:19–21 · altitude map

Far above all rule, authority, power and dominion

Paul uses four words for the power that raised Jesus, then lists five things he is seated far above. Select each layer, and switch between this age and the age to come.

“Far above” (hyperanō) is not just “a little higher”. It means beyond comparison. No rank in the spiritual world, no empire, ideology or name that people invoke comes near him.

The same power now works “toward us who believe” (Eph 1:19). Because we were raised and seated with him (Eph 2:6), what is under his feet is under the feet of his body. We don’t live under the powers. We live from above them, while serving under people in humility.
