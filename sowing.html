John 12:24 · 1 Corinthians 15:20

The seed that died and multiplied

The whole principle finds its centre in Jesus. He is the promised seed of the woman (Gen 3:15) and the grain of wheat: “unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.”

Buried on Friday, he rose as “the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep” (1 Cor 15:20). Every believer is part of the harvest of that one seed, and Jesus sends us into fields “white for harvest” (John 4:35).

2 Corinthians 9:6–15 · cycle diagram

How sowing connects to blessing

In Hebrew, to bless (bārak) is to empower to be fruitful (Gen 1:22, 28). Blessing starts with God’s grace and ends in thanksgiving to God, flowing through us to others. Select a stage.

The great exchange

When grace interrupts the harvest

If sowing and reaping were the whole story, no one could stand. At the cross Christ reaped the harvest of our bad seed, and gave us the harvest of his. Press “Exchange”.

“He was pierced for our transgressions … and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all” (Isa 53:5–6).

“Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us” (Gal 3:13). “He does not deal with us according to our sins” (Ps 103:10).

“Others have laboured, and you have entered into their labour” (John 4:38). This is why biblical sowing is never karma: grace stands at its centre.
